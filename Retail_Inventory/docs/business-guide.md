# Business Guide

How RIIP turns data into decisions and money. Written for a business audience —
no SQL or DAX required.

## The problem it solves

Retailers lose money two ways at once: **capital trapped** in stock that isn't
selling, and **sales lost** when the right stock isn't there. Manual spreadsheets
can't see either clearly or quickly. RIIP gives every stakeholder a trusted,
daily view of both.

## The metrics that matter, in plain English

| Metric | What it really tells you |
|---|---|
| **Inventory Turnover** | How many times a year you sell through your stock. Higher means the same money works harder. |
| **GMROI** | For every $1 of inventory, how much gross margin it earns. Below $1 means the item costs more to hold than it makes. |
| **Days of Supply** | How long until you run out at current demand. Your early-warning gauge. |
| **OTIF** | The share of supplier deliveries that arrive on time *and* complete. The single best measure of supplier reliability. |
| **Dead stock %** | The share of inventory value that hasn't moved in months — trapped cash. |

## How it drives decisions

- **Reorder decisions** — the Exceptions page tells purchasing exactly what to
  reorder today, before a stock-out happens.
- **Supplier decisions** — the scorecard replaces "I think Supplier X is late a
  lot" with a measured OTIF and lead-time ranking for negotiations.
- **Markdown decisions** — dead and overstocked items surface automatically, so
  liquidation is planned, not reactive.
- **Assortment decisions** — ABC/XYZ classification shows which 20% of products
  deserve tight protection and which long tail should be managed for cash.

## The value case

From the analysis of this dataset, a focused programme built entirely on RIIP —
**no new systems** — targets roughly **$0.7–1.0M of annual value**: working
capital released from dead and overstocked inventory, plus margin on sales
recovered by improving availability, minus reduced carrying cost. The quick wins
(a markdown calendar, protecting top-seller availability, the daily exceptions
worklist) self-fund the structural work (velocity-based buying policy, supplier
remediation).

## Why decision-support, not automation

RIIP *recommends*; buyers and the ERP *execute*. That boundary keeps humans in
control of ordering while giving them far better information — lower risk, and a
faster path to value than automating replenishment outright.
